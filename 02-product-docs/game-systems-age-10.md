# Game Systems: Age-10 Quest Structure And Engagement Loop

## Purpose
Turn the approved `Sky of Many Lanterns` direction into:
- a chapter-based plot structure,
- a concrete quest model,
- and engagement systems appropriate for children around age 10.

This document assumes the `9-11` middle-childhood band and should not be generalized to older or younger players without redesign.

## Design Rule
Engagement should come from:
- curiosity,
- competence,
- visible world change,
- narrative payoff,
- and emotional attachment to place and purpose.

Do not rely on:
- anxiety-driven streaks,
- scarcity pressure,
- endless currencies,
- or battle-heavy escalation.

## Macro Structure
Use a 4-region campaign with 3 chapters per region.

That creates:
- 12 major chapters,
- clear medium-term goals,
- frequent unlock moments,
- and enough room for novelty without making the story sprawl.

## Region Structure
Each region follows the same high-level rhythm:

1. Arrival:
   - discover the local problem,
   - meet a new character or companion,
   - learn one new environmental mechanic.
2. Restoration:
   - complete a sequence of expeditions,
   - reopen routes,
   - recover clues and artifacts,
   - understand what caused the disruption.
3. Renewal:
   - resolve a signature landmark,
   - restore local trust and connection,
   - unlock the route to the next region,
   - receive a major story reveal.

This repetition is useful because age-10 players benefit from structural clarity, but each region still needs its own mood and mechanical identity.

## Region Overview
### Region 1: Lantern Reach
Theme:
First belonging, first competence, first route repairs.

Mood:
Golden clouds, village bridges, field stations, welcoming guild spaces.

Gameplay focus:
- basic route restoration,
- clear pattern tasks,
- low-pressure hint discovery,
- first companion unlock.

Narrative purpose:
Teach the child what a Pathfinder is and prove that logical thinking changes the world in visible ways.

### Region 2: The Wind Archive
Theme:
Mystery, lost records, signal decoding.

Mood:
Hanging libraries, weather vanes, moving shelves, echo towers.

Gameplay focus:
- contradiction detection,
- clue comparison,
- map fragments,
- archive key recovery.

Narrative purpose:
Introduce the idea that the lantern network was built with intention and memory.

### Region 3: Stormglass Wilds
Theme:
Courage under uncertainty.

Mood:
Charged skies, storm beacons, fractured observatories, gliding creatures.

Gameplay focus:
- if/then controls,
- chained deductions,
- multi-step expeditions,
- environmental disruptions.

Narrative purpose:
Let the child feel responsible and resilient without making the world frightening.

### Region 4: The High Observatory
Theme:
Stewardship and hope.

Mood:
Ancient towers, star mirrors, guild relics, long-distance signals.

Gameplay focus:
- integrated mixed-skill missions,
- artifact synthesis,
- final route relighting,
- community reconnection scenes.

Narrative purpose:
Reveal the original purpose of the network and let the child complete a meaningful act of restoration.

## Chapter Structure
Each chapter should contain:
- 4-6 regular expeditions,
- 1 story expedition,
- 1 landmark restoration moment,
- 1 reward scene or guild-base upgrade.

This means the player gets:
- daily progression through normal sessions,
- weekly-feeling payoffs through chapter completions,
- and a larger campaign cadence across regions.

## Daily Expedition Model
Each daily expedition should have this shape:

1. Mission briefing:
   - short,
   - specific,
   - contextualized by the world.
2. Field challenge set:
   - 5-7 logic tasks,
   - skill-weighted to current learner needs.
3. Mid-session event:
   - a route flicker,
   - a clue discovery,
   - a companion observation,
   - or a machine response.
4. Completion scene:
   - visual world repair,
   - chapter progress,
   - and one line of emotionally warm payoff.
5. Return hook:
   - tomorrow's next destination,
   - a partially revealed mystery,
   - or a nearly completed landmark.

## Quest Types
Use a mix of quest types so progression does not feel repetitive.

### 1. Route Quests
Purpose:
Reconnect islands and unlock traversal.

Best skill fits:
- patterns
- deduction

Payoff:
New map routes and visible travel lines.

### 2. Repair Quests
Purpose:
Fix beacons, bridges, elevators, archives, and signal devices.

Best skill fits:
- if/then
- deduction

Payoff:
Mechanical spectacle and satisfying world-state change.

### 3. Investigation Quests
Purpose:
Resolve conflicting notes, missing records, or strange failures.

Best skill fits:
- contradiction
- classification

Payoff:
Mystery progression and story clarity.

### 4. Recovery Quests
Purpose:
Find artifacts, journal pages, and companion traces.

Best skill fits:
- classification
- patterns

Payoff:
Lore and collectible completion.

### 5. Service Quests
Purpose:
Help a village, workshop, or traveler.

Best skill fits:
- mixed

Payoff:
Human meaning.
These quests are especially important because they stop the game from feeling like abstract infrastructure repair.

## Signature Set Pieces
Each region should contain one strong age-10 fantasy moment.

Examples:
- relighting a suspended bridge in the clouds
- decoding a tower that sings in color pulses
- steering a storm beacon back into balance
- restoring the observatory so distant islands can see each other again

These moments act like "bosses," but they should be puzzle culminations, not combat encounters.

## Engagement Systems
### 1. Lantern Map Completion
Primary long-term engagement system.

What it does:
- shows restored routes,
- shows fogged future areas,
- highlights one next destination,
- and makes progress spatial and memorable.

Why it works:
Age-10 children strongly respond to visible unlocks and "I opened that part" ownership.

### 2. Journal Of Discoveries
A collectible story log with:
- recovered notes,
- creature sketches,
- route symbols,
- guild history,
- and child-earned reflections.

Why it works:
Supports curiosity and completion motivation without leaning on scarcity tricks.

### 3. Guild Base Upgrades
Every major milestone upgrades a shared home space.

Upgrade examples:
- workshop tools
- observatory lens array
- map wall pins
- greenhouse glow vines
- companion roosts

Why it works:
Creates emotional stability and a sense of stewardship.

### 4. Companion Bond Moments
Companions should unlock through story progress and occasionally appear:
- during briefings,
- at session completion,
- or when a region landmark is restored.

Do not make them a pet-maintenance system.
The bond should feel warm, not needy.

### 5. Mastery Badges
For each logic strand, award visible guild emblems when the child shows steady competence.

Examples:
- Pattern Reader
- Route Keeper
- Signal Solver
- Beacon Repairer
- True Note Finder

Why it works:
Age-10 players like identity-through-competence.
Badges should feel earned and meaningful, not spammy.

### 6. Weekly Guild Report
This is the parent-facing mirror of child progress.

It should convert game progress into plain language:
- what region moved forward,
- what skill improved,
- what challenge still needs support,
- and what landmark was restored this week.

This turns game engagement into parent trust.

## Reward Economy
Use only a small set of currencies and make each one legible.

### Primary Reward: Lantern Charge
Earned from completed sessions.
Used to restore routes and power landmark scenes.

Meaning:
Represents the child's effort and clear thinking.

### Secondary Reward: Guild Tokens
Earned from chapter completions and service quests.
Used only for cosmetic camp upgrades or companion accessories.

Meaning:
Optional personalization, not progression gating.

### Major Reward: Artifacts
Unique chapter or region rewards.
Never sold, randomized, or spammed.

Meaning:
Story milestones and identity anchors.

## Safe Retention Hooks
Use these:
- visible unfinished landmarks
- tomorrow preview scenes
- one mystery question per chapter
- new island silhouettes on the map
- companion return moments

Avoid these:
- fear of losing progress
- long punitive absences
- "come back now or miss out"
- variable-ratio prize spam
- fake social pressure

## Difficulty And Momentum Rules
### Momentum Protection
If a child struggles:
- shorten the remaining mission,
- increase support,
- show a partial success outcome,
- and still advance some world state.

### Confidence Ramps
Open each new chapter with:
- one easy win,
- one familiar task frame,
- one new flourish.

### Landmark Readiness
Landmark missions should trigger only when recent success suggests the child is ready for a slightly bigger challenge.

## Narrative Hook Grid
Use one hook per chapter so motivation stays clear.

Hook types:
- "What is causing this failure?"
- "Who left this message?"
- "What lies beyond the unopened route?"
- "Can we help this place recover in time?"
- "What was the network originally for?"

## Example Chapter Arc
### Region 1, Chapter 2: The Stair Of Singing Ropes
Start:
The child learns that supply gliders cannot reach a nearby village.

Expeditions:
- decode rope-light patterns
- compare false repair notes
- sort the right gear parts
- infer the correct restart order

Story beat:
The clockwork bird companion returns with a missing route marker.

Landmark finish:
The stair hums back to life and ribbons of light stretch between islands.

Reward:
- new map section,
- one lantern lens artifact,
- guild workshop upgrade,
- parent report note: "showed stronger pattern recognition today."

## Recommendation
Build the age-10 content roadmap around:
- 4 regions,
- 12 chapters,
- expedition-based daily sessions,
- map restoration as the main macro loop,
- and story-rich but calm puzzle set pieces instead of combat bosses.

This gives the product a strong long-term engagement structure while staying consistent with the educational mission and emotional safety requirements.
