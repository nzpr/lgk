# Game Direction: Age-10 Adventure Loop

## Target Age Band
For this direction, target children around age 10 using the `9-11` middle-childhood band.

This is a useful design band because children in this range typically want:
- more autonomy than younger children,
- visible competence and mastery,
- richer stories with stakes and mystery,
- and social/emotional meaning without adolescent cynicism or romance.

For later work, use this practical working split:
- `6-8`: early school-age
- `9-11`: middle childhood
- `12-14`: early adolescence

The exact boundaries vary across frameworks, but `9-11` is the right band for the current concept.

## Design Goals For Age 10
- Make the child feel capable, brave, and clever.
- Replace "school task" energy with "expedition problem-solving" energy.
- Use adventure stakes that feel meaningful but not scary.
- Let progress feel earned through reasoning, not grinding.
- Keep the emotional tone inspiring, warm, and forward-looking.

## Core Game Premise
The player is a young Pathfinder restoring the `Sky of Many Lanterns`.

Long ago, guiding lanterns connected islands across the sky.
Now the lantern routes are dimming, maps are incomplete, and whole regions are drifting apart.
The child joins a small exploration guild whose job is to relight routes, recover lost knowledge, and help sky communities reconnect.

Every reasoning session powers one real expedition step:
- decode a route,
- reopen a bridge,
- repair a signal tower,
- calm a mechanical storm beacon,
- recover an explorer's journal,
- or bring light back to a village.

The fantasy is not "be the chosen one."
The fantasy is:
"You are becoming the kind of person who can see patterns, stay calm, and help others find the way."

## Plot Frame
### World
A chain of floating islands, observatories, wind forests, beacon towers, hidden archives, and quiet villages above the clouds.

### Player Role
Junior Pathfinder in the Lantern Guild.

### Mentor Cast
- `Tala`: calm expedition guide who frames missions warmly and clearly.
- `Orin`: eccentric mapmaker who loves patterns and hidden routes.
- `Mira`: young engineer who turns the child's solved problems into repaired devices and reopened pathways.

### Long Arc
The guild learns that the lantern network is not failing randomly.
Its routes were designed to respond to clear thinking, cooperation, and remembered knowledge.
The child slowly restores one region at a time and uncovers the original purpose of the network:
to help distant communities share wisdom during uncertain times.

### Emotional Arc
- Early game: curiosity and first competence.
- Mid game: courage and responsibility.
- Later game: hope, stewardship, and the feeling that their thinking changes the world for the better.

## Session-To-Story Loop
Each daily session should map to one narrative beat:

1. The child receives a small expedition objective.
2. They solve 5-7 reasoning tasks framed as fieldwork.
3. Each solved task restores part of a route, mechanism, or clue trail.
4. Session end reveals a visible world change:
   - a lantern relights,
   - a path extends,
   - a creature guide returns,
   - a map piece unlocks,
   - or a village message arrives.
5. The child leaves with a reason to come back tomorrow.

This keeps plot advancement concrete and earned.

## Core Mechanics
### 1. Expedition Sessions
The daily session is the main mode.
Each session is a short expedition segment with 5-7 logic challenges.

Why it fits age 10:
- clear short-term goals,
- visible progress,
- repeatable structure with new context,
- enough challenge to feel real.

### 2. Route Restoration Map
Completing sessions restores visible routes across a sky map.

Player payoff:
- unlock new islands,
- reveal landmarks,
- open side stories,
- gain visual proof of competence.

Why it fits age 10:
- children in this range respond well to map progress and "I unlocked that" ownership.

### 3. Artifact Recovery
Major milestones award artifacts:
- compass shards,
- lantern lenses,
- old guild badges,
- journal pages,
- signal crystals.

Artifacts are not just collectibles.
Each one expands the story or adds a practical game bonus such as:
- one extra hint use,
- preview of a mission theme,
- special cosmetic lantern glow,
- or access to a short lore scene.

### 4. Companion Guides
The child gradually befriends non-player companions:
- a glider fox,
- a clockwork bird,
- a cloud moth,
- a bridge-tending turtle.

Companions do not talk excessively.
They give emotional warmth, mission flavor, and occasional contextual encouragement.

Why it fits age 10:
- companionship adds attachment without turning the experience into needy chatbot behavior.

### 5. Camp Upgrade Loop
The guild base improves as the child progresses:
- map wall fills in,
- workshop gains tools,
- observatory lights up,
- library shelves repopulate,
- garden grows.

This gives a stable home feeling and reinforces long-term progress.

### 6. Calm Stakes, Not Punishment
Failure should never destroy progress.
Instead:
- storms slow travel,
- a signal flickers,
- a route remains incomplete,
- a clue stays unclear.

The child is invited to try again with support.

That keeps the tone adventurous but emotionally safe.

## Task Framing By Skill
Tie the learning system to mission verbs:

- `classification`: sort supplies, creatures, symbols, or star samples
- `patterns`: decode lantern pulse sequences and route rhythms
- `if/then`: follow machine rules, weather rules, and bridge controls
- `contradiction`: spot false field notes or conflicting witness reports
- `deduction`: infer the right path, tool, or repair step from gathered clues

This makes reasoning feel useful inside the world.

## Reward Model
Use layered rewards:

- `Immediate`: satisfying feedback, animation, warm encouragement
- `Session`: route progress, lantern relight, clue reveal
- `Short arc`: new island, artifact, companion scene
- `Long arc`: restore a full region of the sky network

Avoid:
- loud random loot,
- endless currencies,
- manipulative streak pressure,
- overblown battle framing.

## Tone Rules
- adventurous, not aggressive
- inspiring, not preachy
- warm, not babyish
- mysterious, not frightening
- hopeful, not saccharine

The child should feel respected.
At age 10, the world should feel bigger than them, but they should still believe they can matter in it.

## Example Episode
### Episode: The Broken Wind Stair
Problem:
A stair-bridge between two islands only opens when its lantern code is repaired.

Session beats:
1. Tala explains that nearby families cannot trade until the route reopens.
2. The child solves pattern and deduction tasks to restore the code.
3. Midway, a storm gust disrupts the stair and introduces an if/then puzzle.
4. Final task resolves a contradiction between two old route notes.
5. The stair lights up, the clouds part, and a new island becomes reachable.

Ending feeling:
"I used my brain to help people and open the world."

## What To Avoid For This Age
- bleak apocalypse framing
- sarcasm-heavy writing
- romance or identity drama
- dense political lore
- purely decorative progression with no world effect
- battle systems that overpower the reasoning loop

## Recommendation
Use `Sky of Many Lanterns` as the default age-10 plot direction.

It supports:
- adventurous exploration,
- calm emotional warmth,
- clear mission framing,
- visible progression,
- and a direct connection between logical thinking and meaningful change.
